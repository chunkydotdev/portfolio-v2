## The Only Two Numbers That Matter

Every successful indie dev I studied obsessed over the same ratio:

**Popularity** = How many people search for this keyword
**Difficulty** = How hard it is to rank for it

The sweet spot everyone agreed on: **Popularity 15-40, Difficulty under 60.**

- Under 10 popularity? Don't bother. Not enough traffic.
- Over 70 difficulty? You're competing with apps that have million-dollar marketing budgets.

This ratio determines whether your app gets discovered or dies in obscurity.

## The 4 Tools That Keep Coming Up

Every tutorial, every successful indie dev interview — the same tools appeared over and over:

### 1. Astro (The Keyword Bible)

Astro pulls data from Apple's ad bidding system. It shows you exactly what people search for and how competitive those terms are.

The process that kept appearing:

1. Add a competitor app
2. Steal their ranking keywords
3. Find gaps where popularity is decent but difficulty is low

**Example from one dev's breakdown:**

"Playlist transfer" (Spotify to Apple Music migration)
- Popularity: 33
- Difficulty: 25
- Verdict: Could generate $500/month

vs. "Music transfer"
- Popularity: 30
- Difficulty: 78
- Same search intent, 3x harder to rank

The insight: Similar keywords can have wildly different difficulty scores. Most devs never check — they just guess.

**The hack:** If apps ranking #2-5 don't have your exact keyword in their title, you can often outrank them just by naming your app properly.

---

### 2. Google Trends (Catch Waves Early)

Set it to:
- United States
- Past 90 days
- Focus on "rising queries"

One dev found "shadow work journal" trending on TikTok before it hit the App Store. Books were selling, influencers were posting, but barely any apps existed.

- Popularity: 21
- Difficulty: 56
- Target audience willing to pay for subscriptions

**The play:** Social platforms show trends 3-6 months before the App Store gets saturated. Google Trends is your early warning system.

---

### 3. X/Twitter (Complaints = Opportunities)

Search for:
- "wish there was an app for"
- "why isn't there an iPhone app that"
- "hate how [category] works"

People literally tell you what to build.

But here's the insight that separated the successful devs from everyone else:

**Don't build what they ask for. Build what they actually need.**

One dev shared this story: At a party, someone said "I wish I could play piano like that."

Surface level: Piano learning app.
**Actual need:** Learn ONE impressive song in a weekend to show off at parties. No music theory. No commitment.

The apps that win understand the psychology behind the request.

---

### 4. Reddit (Niche Validation)

Subreddits to mine:
- r/apps
- r/productivity
- r/ios
- Any niche-specific community

Search for posts with high engagement about app frustrations. These communities tell you exactly what features existing apps are missing.

**Bonus:** Build what they ask for, then post your solution. Built-in launch audience.

## The Validation Framework Everyone Uses

Before writing code, successful devs check:

1. **Popularity 15+** in Astro
2. **Difficulty under 60** (under 40 is ideal)
3. **No dominant player** — fragmented top 10
4. **Daily use case** — subscriptions need habits
5. **Rising or evergreen** — not a 3-month fad

Fail any of these? Move on. Ideas are infinite. Time isn't.

## The Portfolio Strategy Nobody Talks About

The devs making real money aren't chasing one viral app.

They're stacking small wins.

One keyword with popularity 25 and low difficulty = maybe $500/month.

Not life-changing. But:
- 5 apps = $2,500/month
- 10 apps = $5,000/month
- 20 apps = $10,000/month

Each app takes 2-4 weeks to build. The math works if you pick the right keywords.

**The mindset shift:** You're not building products. You're building a keyword portfolio.
